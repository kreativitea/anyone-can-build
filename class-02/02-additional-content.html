Assigned after class, on top of the syllabus outside-work. Short follow-up (basically light homework): do it before next week.

Read

  • MDN, What is a URL? (~10 min). You now own an address, and you sent it to someone. This takes that address apart, piece by piece, so none of it is a guess. Read the part about the path, and the part about absolute and relative links: that difference is the usual reason a page that worked on your laptop shows "not found" after you publish it — and it is why we kept everything in one file today.
  • CERN, the first web page (~2 min). Your page went up this afternoon; this one went up in 1991 and is still at its original address. Look at how little is there — plain text and links, one job, one screen. The first web page anyone ever published was smaller than yours.
  • Randall Munroe, Digital Resource Lifespan (xkcd 1909, ~30 seconds). One drawing, about how long things on the internet really last. This afternoon you published an address and gave it to somebody. Will it still work in a year? And who decides that — you, or someone else?
