Class 1 — Additional content

Posted at the end of the Friday, together with Class 2's page — homework is batched at the end of the even class, never between the two periods of one afternoon. Short follow-up (basically light homework): do it before next week.

Read

  • Andrej Karpathy, "vibe coding" (Feb 2025, ~1 min). A few sentences, written by one of the people who built modern AI, naming a way of working: say what you want, take what comes back, and do not read the code. He is describing throwaway weekend projects — which is exactly what today was. The question to carry into next week is where the line sits: what would you need to understand before you would put your name on it?
  • Titus Winters, Tom Manshreck and Hyrum Wright, Software Engineering at Google, chapter 1 (~10 min if you stop where we say). Read the first section, "What Is Software Engineering?", and stop when you reach "Time and Change." If you want the rest of the chapter in one minute, jump to "TL;DRs" at the very bottom. This is where today's definition comes from — software engineering is programming integrated over time — written by engineers who keep one of the largest collections of code in the world working. The two words worth thinking about are over time. Almost everything this course asks of you lives in them.
  • Paul Graham, Hackers and Painters (~25 min). Software as craft — making things, taste, and learning by doing — from a programmer who argues his field looks more like painting than like science. A good companion to today's question of what stays yours when the machine writes the code.

Watch

  • Josh Darnit, Exact Instructions Challenge — "THIS is why my kids hate me" (~5 min). A dad makes a peanut-butter-and-jelly sandwich by following his kids' written instructions literally. The funniest possible version of today's point: a computer does exactly, and only, what you say, so every ambiguity becomes a bug. Watch for where each instruction breaks, and how they debug it.
