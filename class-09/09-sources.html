Class 9 — Sources

Not homework. These are the sources behind Class 9's claims, so that any of them can be checked. Most of the class is the course's own material: the restaurant floor plan, the earthquake map and Timeline were made for the course. What comes from outside is three real building plans, and the words the field uses for what the class taught.

Three blueprints

  • Historic American Buildings Survey (HABS), National Park Service, kept at the Library of Congress — the three floor plans on Blueprint A, Blueprint B and Blueprint C, each cropped to the building and recoloured as a blueprint, with its room names covered and written again:
  • Conrad and Nellie Warren Residence, Deer Lodge, Montana (HABS MT-107, sheet 2, First Floor Plan), drawn for the National Park Service by Joseph Thomas and Todd Delyea (2001–2002) — the house.
  • Samuel Findley School, Akron, Ohio (HABS OH-2480, sheet 4, First Floor Plan), drawn by Carly Butcher and Tim Coerver of Kent State University — the school.
  • Big Ernie's Fabulous Fifties Diner, Wildwood, New Jersey (HABS NJ-1244, sheet 3, Original Plan), drawn by Jim Garrison and Brian Meng — the restaurant, and the small plan on What architecture is.

The Library of Congress pages refuse automated requests, so they were read through the drawings' own files; they open normally in a browser.

  • National Park Service, Charles E. Peterson Prize — the school and the diner were drawn by students, as entries to this annual HABS drawing competition (2008 and 2000).

Separation of concerns

  • Edsger W. Dijkstra, On the role of scientific thought (EWD 447, 1974) — the essay usually given as the origin of the phrase separation of concerns, the rule on What architecture is and Separation of concerns. In it he says he has "sometimes called" it that, so he may have used it earlier. A short essay, readable without programming.
  • IBM, What is three-tier architecture? — the presentation, application and data tiers, and the rule that the presentation tier never talks to the data tier directly, on Three-tier architecture and The kitchen, with a third room. Nobody can be credited with the term three-tier: who coined it could not be established. Also on the class page.

Timeline

  • Timeline — the app on every demo slide, written for the course. Its code is the source for Timeline's blueprint, the request and response slides, and the rows on Open the database.

Database

  • E. F. Codd, A Relational Model of Data for Large Shared Data Banks (Communications of the ACM, June 1970) — the paper behind tables, rows and keys as databases use them today, and where the word normalization first appears, behind Database normalization. There it means flattening nested data; each fact stored once, the sense the slide uses, came with the normal forms he set out in the years after. Free as a scan; ACM's page refused the fetcher.
