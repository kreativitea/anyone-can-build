The four checks, for each service

A service here is a part of your app that another company runs: Supabase, Render, Sentry. In class, each service had a slide with the same four checks. Answer them for Render and for Sentry, and write each answer in your tracker:

  1. Can Claude connect to this service? Claude connects through the service's MCP server (Class 17's page says what MCP is). In Claude Code, /mcp lists the services Claude can reach.
  2. Which keys must I put in by hand? Keys are always yours to put in, even when Claude is connected.
  3. What can Claude do after that? Often the rest, the deployment too.
  4. Ask Claude: what do I have to do by hand here, and what can you do?

Do: finish any gate you did not reach

A gate was a checkpoint in class: everybody stopped there until the room was ready. There were three. Gate A was Supabase's row-level security, which is step 3 on Class 17's page. Finish the other two, in this order, because Gate C needs Gate B.

Gate B: your app, live, on your phone

Render runs your app on the internet, so that anyone can open it at a URL. Your app is a static site: Render only sends your files to the browser, and the code runs in the browser.

  1. By hand, on Render's website: sign in to Render with GitHub. Render asks to install its GitHub app, which is how Render reads your code. Choose Only select repositories, and pick your app's repository.
  2. By hand: connect Render's MCP server. On render.com/docs/mcp-server, follow the steps for Claude Code. Then type /mcp in Claude Code: the list should show render.
  3. Ask Claude: put my app on Render as a static site.
  4. If Claude cannot do a step, do it on Render's website: click New, then Static Site, then choose your repository. For Publish Directory, type the name of the folder that has your index.html.
  5. Render gives your app a URL that ends in onrender.com. Open it on your phone's browser, and save something. Then find that row in your Supabase Table Editor.

Gate C: an error from your phone, in Sentry

Sentry tells you when your app breaks on someone else's device. When an error happens, your page sends Sentry a report of it.

  1. By hand, on Sentry's website: make a free Sentry account, then a project. For the platform, choose Browser JavaScript.
  2. By hand: connect Sentry's MCP server. On mcp.sentry.dev, follow the steps for Claude Code. Then type /mcp in Claude Code: the list should show sentry.
  3. By hand: copy your project's DSN into the same config file as your Supabase values. Sentry shows the DSN when you make the project; later you find it in the project's settings, under Client Keys (DSN). A DSN is the address your page sends its errors to. It is safe for anyone to see, like the Publishable key, because it only lets someone send new errors. It does not let anyone read them. (The Supabase Secret key is different: that one never goes anywhere.)
  4. Ask Claude: add Sentry's Loader Script to my page, before all the other scripts. The Loader Script is the small piece of code that sends errors to Sentry.
  5. Ask Claude: add one line that breaks my app on purpose, so I can see the error in Sentry. Commit and push. Your app is broken until step 7, so do steps 5 to 7 at one time.
  6. Open your app on your phone, so that the error happens there. Then ask Claude: find this error in Sentry.
  7. Ask Claude to remove the line that breaks your app. Commit and push again. Open your app on your phone and check that it works.

Do: write what Sentry sends

Ask Claude: when an error happens, what does Sentry send, and is any of it personal information? Personal information is anything that can tell who a person is, such as a name, an email address or an IP address. Then open the error in your Sentry project and check Claude's answer against what you see. Write the answer in your tracker, in your own words.
